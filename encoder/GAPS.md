# Encoder: datasheet gaps

What the reference datasheet (`docs-tech/tms5220_reference_datasheet/tms5220_reference_datasheet.md`) left unclear, ambiguous or missing for someone writing an encoder from it alone, and what `tms5220enc.py` chose. Section numbers are the datasheet's. Items marked **Error** are statements I believe are wrong.

## Status

The datasheet was revised after the first version of this list. Items 1–4, 8, 9, 21, 22, 25, 26 and 29 are now resolved in the text, each in the way the encoder had chosen. §12.2 now also gives the frame-to-output mapping of item 6 (sample 225 + 200·*j*). Item 31, found afterwards, is now covered in §12.2 too, and the encoder handles it. The other items are still open.

## Synthesis (§8.5–§8.9)

My `synthesize()` follows §8.9 step by step. It gives the reference emulator's codes sample for sample on random speech-like frame lists and on encoded speech, through the VSM path (`test_tms5220enc.py`). Getting there took one bug fix, and these points were where the text could be read more than one way:

1. **Resolved in the text** (§8.9 "Loading a frame" now says idx[2..5] only, idx[6..11] kept). **Unvoiced frames and the K5–K10 indices (§8.9 "Loading a frame").** "idx[2...] = the frame's K indices (K1–K4, or K1–K10 if voiced)" can be read as "an unvoiced frame sets K5–K10 to index 0". I first read it that way, and my output differed from the emulator after the next silence frame. Only §8.7 ("the table values at their stored indices, which an unvoiced frame never changes") settles it. *Suggest:* "an unvoiced frame writes idx[2..5] only; idx[6..11] keep their values".
2. **Resolved in the text** (§8.9 step 6 now says every sample period). **The pitch counter step (§8.9 step 6).** "Then, if talking: count = count + 1 …" sits under "End of the period. If this is PC 12 of IP 7". It is not clear whether it runs in every sample period or only in frame-read samples. *Chose:* every sample period. That is the only reading that gives a pitch at all, and it matches the emulator.
3. **Resolved in the text** (§8.9 step 3 now says the write is applied before step 4). **Writes due "in this sample" (§8.9 step 3).** When not talking, a K is zeroed "at n + d(p) − 1, that is in this sample". Step 1, which applies queued writes, has already run by then. *Chose:* apply due writes again just before the filter (step 4).
4. **Resolved in the text** (§8.9 step 2.4 now says "as it was during sample n − 1, before that sample's step 6"). **"oldU as it was in sample n − 1" (§8.9 step 2.4).** *Chose:* the value during sample n − 1, before that sample's end-of-period update. This gives the "from sample 2 on" of §8.5.
5. **Which noise state to use.** `synthesize()` models one VSM Speak with the standard procedure: register 0x00EB at slot 0 of sample 1 (§7), from power-up state. It does not model the Speak External path, which runs 2 samples earlier with other noise (§7, §8.5). The encoder only needs levels, so this does not matter for encoding. It does mean my synthesis of unvoiced frames is exact for one start time only.

## Timing, for an author (§12.2, §12.6)

6. **Which output samples belong to which frame.** §12.2 says frame *n* describes the sound at 25·*n* ms, and its values are reached in IP 0 of the next frame (samples 201–225 after its read). For level setting and measurement I needed one output sample to stand for each frame. *Chose:* sample 225 + 200·*j* from the first frame read. That is the middle of the stretch where frame *j*'s values dominate: half-way into its own glide at the end of IP 4 (sample 125), half-way to the next frame's values by sample 325. The input frame centres are then `onset + 22 + 200·j`, because the sound starts at output sample 203 (energy computed at 201, used from 203, §8.5). *Suggest:* the datasheet could state this mapping, with the asymmetry of the glide (0.5 of the way after 100 samples).
7. **Measuring a frame's level (§12.6 steps 3 and 5).** Because of the glide, a 25–40 ms output window around a frame also holds a large share of its neighbours. A per-frame closed loop then mistakes a neighbour's level for the frame's own. Early versions pushed decaying tails down to energy 1 and then to silence. *Chose:* the same Hann-weighted 25 ms window on both sides (input centred on the frame centre, output on sample 225 + 200·*j*), with a damped closed loop through the reference algorithm. §12.6 step 5 could warn about this.

## Levels (§12.3, §12.6)

8. **Resolved in the text** (§12.3 now says the peak limit wins over an RMS target, and gives 15–20 codes at ±120 peaks). **RMS target and peak limit conflict.** §12.3 asks for peaks within ±127 and an RMS like the originals' (19–20 codes). §12.6 step 5 says to scale the whole utterance once, so that its loudest frame peaks within ±127. On these inputs, a single scale that puts the loudest output peak at 120 gives a median output RMS of 15.9 codes (40 files, `targetPeak=120`). The RMS goal needs about 20. *Chose:* the RMS target of 20 codes, plus a per-frame limiter: any frame whose output peaks above 120 has its energy index lowered. This acts on a minority of frames (see README, "Quality"). The result measures 18.4 codes by the same measure. `targetPeak` is available as an option for the other reading. The datasheet does not say which of the two goals wins, or whether the originals were limited.
9. **Resolved in the text** (§12.3 now warns that a resonant filter can make index 1 too loud, and to check each frame by synthesis). **The quietest frame depends on the filter (§12.3 "The energy steps").** "Index 1 is 41 dB below index 14. A frame quieter than that is silence" treats energy as the level. But the filter's gain varies by more than 40 dB from frame to frame. An unvoiced frame with K1 = −501 (a near-DC resonance) peaked at 179 codes at energy index 1. *Chose:* a frame still more than 12 dB above its target at index 1 becomes silence. So does a frame the limiter cannot bring below the peak limit at index 1. A repeat frame that relied on a silenced frame's K is sent as a full frame.
10. **The silence threshold (§12.6 step 6).** "Below a level threshold" gives no threshold. *Chose:* 45 dB below the loudest frame (4 dB below what energy index 1 can reach from index 14), or twice the noise floor (the 5th percentile of 10 ms levels), whichever is higher.
11. **Wrapping inside the lattice.** §8.8 says every sum wraps at 14 bits, while §12.3 discusses only the output (±512 codes). It does not say whether internal *u*ᵢ and *b*ᵢ can wrap while *u*₁ stays small. *Chose:* the synthesis checks every sum. The encoder lowers energies until none wraps. No encoded input wrapped at a 120-code peak limit.
12. **Margin for the chip.** The chip differs from the reference algorithm by about one code, more in resonant frames, and has upsets (§7, §8.8). The datasheet does not say how much margin an author should leave below ±127. *Chose:* a 120-code peak limit.

## Frame kinds (§12.2, §12.5)

13. **"Close" K indices for a repeat (§12.5).** This is not quantified. *Chose:* repeats are chosen by a rate-distortion search within each run of one voicing: bits + `repeatLambda` (70) × the rise in each frame's prediction error, in dB, under the repeated K. This is a likelihood-ratio (Itakura) measure. Repeats only follow a full frame of the same voicing. (Before: a greedy rule, a repeat when the stored K cost at most 0.8 dB; see README.)
14. **Unvoiced repeat after a voiced frame.** §12.5 says this is allowed (K1–K4 kept, K5–K10 zeroed), but it never says when it is useful. *Chose:* never, apart from the fades below.
15. **How to fade a hiss (§12.2).** "Step its energy down over a few unvoiced repeat frames first" leaves the rate open. *Chose:* each fade frame has a third of the previous energy value, until the value is 2 or less (for example 33 → 11 → 4 → 1). Inside an utterance, fade frames replace the silence frames after the hiss. At the end, they are added before the stop.
16. **Leading silence.** §12.2 says to start with a voiced or unvoiced frame. *Chose:* after level setting, the encoder drops any leading silence frames, so the first frame always loads every index.

## Analysis (§12.3, §12.6)

17. **Pre-emphasis 0.9 (§12.3).** I checked the stated chirp spectrum (+3.9, −8.8, −11.7, −17.6 dB at 100, 500, 1000 and 2000 Hz relative to 250 Hz). It is right to 0.1 dB, and 1 − 0.9 z⁻¹ inverts it well. Trying 0.8 and 0.95 changed my spectral distance by less than 0.05 dB, so I kept 0.9. *Later:* the order-4 LPC inverse of the chirp table replaced it, because it also matches the chirp's shape from 300 to 2000 Hz and halves the output's tilt error (README, Pre-emphasis).
18. **Window (§12.6 step 3: 25–40 ms).** *Chose:* 30 ms Hamming. For frame 0, whose centre is at the onset, the spectral window is moved to start near the onset, so it does not analyse mostly silence.
19. **Quantisation (§12.6 step 4).** The text says nearest in LAR. *Chose:* nearest in LAR, then a coordinate search over ±2 indices of each K that minimises the prediction error of the quantised filter on the frame's autocorrelation.
20. **Voicing and pitch (§12.6 step 3).** The datasheet gives no method and no warning about octave errors. That is encoder craft, not a gap in the chip's description. My choices are in README.md.

## Errors

21. **Resolved in the text** (§12.4 now says indices 1–28). **Error, §12.4:** "Indices 1–30 step one sample at a time (3–6 % at 16–31 samples)." In Table 8-5 the period steps by one sample only for indices 1–28 (periods 16–43, steps of 6.3 % down to 2.4 %). Index 29 is 45 and index 30 is 47 samples. Periods 16–31 are indices 1–16. *Suggest:* "Indices 1–28 step one sample at a time (6 % at 16 samples, 2.4 % at 42); above that the steps are 2–6 samples, about 4–5 %."
22. **Resolved in the text** (§12.10 now gives a median of 79 samples (101 Hz)). **Error (minor), §12.10:** "a median of 44 … with a median of 81 samples (99 Hz)". Index 44 has a period of 79 samples (101 Hz); 81 samples is index 45. Because the index-to-period map is monotonic, the two medians should agree.
23. *Checked, correct:* the glide fractions of §12.2 (0.125 … 0.906); the energy-step figures of §12.3; the chirp energy of 65,546 and its RMS at *P* = 16, 100 and 160; the lattice sign convention of §12.3 (`test_tms5220enc.py`, one-stage check).

## Packaging (§12.7–§12.9)

24. **Acorn PHROM: ASCII entries with no word.** The format reserves numbers 32–126 for "ASCII associated" entries, but the datasheet does not say what an image without a word for a character should hold. *Chose:* always 95 ASCII entries, so word 127's pointer is at byte 254 as in the Acorn PHROM A. Unmapped entries point to one shared byte holding a lone stop code (0x0F), placed after the last word. Speaking it does nothing (§4: a first-frame stop gives no output).
25. **Resolved in the text** (§12.8 now defines the pointer count as including the ASCII entries (260 = 95 + 165)). **Acorn PHROM: the pointer count.** "The number of pointers (260)" is not defined. I inferred 95 ASCII entries + 165 words, so I write 95 + the number of words.
26. **Resolved in the text** (§12.8 now says every header byte is stored bit-reversed, the counts included). **Acorn PHROM: which header bytes are bit-reversed.** The table heading says bytes 0–63 are "stored bit-reversed". *Chose:* every header byte is bit-reversed, the 16-bit counts and bytes 60–63 (0x00, 0x02, 0x00, 0x02 as the host reads them) included. If the counts were meant to be stored plain, the image is wrong there. The 0xFF after the last pointer is the same either way.
27. **Acorn PHROM: where a name starts.** Names are stored backwards before the data, "shifted left by one, then bit-reversed". The datasheet does not say how a reader finds the first byte of a name (the far end), or what the freed bit is for. It may be an end-of-name flag. *Chose:* no flag bits. Names directly follow the previous word's stop byte, or the pointer table's 0xFF for the first word, as described. No spare byte is left after a word's stop code, since the datasheet mentions one only for TI images. Unused bytes are 0x00.
28. **Acorn PHROM: untested.** As the datasheet says, whether a BBC Micro reads a new image in this format has not been tested.
29. **Resolved in the text** (§12.8 now says the TI count byte is stored as is, not bit-reversed). **TI indexed: is the count byte bit-reversed?** §12.7 says headers and counts meant for the host are stored bit-reversed. §12.8 (and `proms/README.md`) give "byte 1 is the number of words" with no reversal. *Chose:* stored plain, as §12.8 states it. Words are numbered from 1, at most 255. Pointers are 14-bit offsets, low byte first. The image is 16 KB, zero-filled.
30. **Speak External padding (§12.9).** Silence frames before the stop keep the stream at 9 bytes or more. If the last sound frame is unvoiced, those silence frames end it abruptly, because unvoiced to silence is inhibited (§8.7). The encoder's own output already fades a final hiss. A stream that is only a stop code becomes 16 silence frames and a stop: 0.4 s of nothing, starting with a silence frame, whose start rule is approximate (§7). The datasheet could say whether padding is better placed somewhere else.

## Found later

31. **Utterances that depend on the previous utterance (now §12.2, "K5–K10 before the first voiced frame").** An unvoiced frame writes only K1–K4 indices. Until the first full voiced frame, the stored K5–K10 indices are the previous utterance's. A silence frame ends the K5–K10 zeroing, so an utterance that opens with an unvoiced sound and then has silence before any voiced frame filters its decay with the predecessor's K5–K10. This was not stated before. I missed it because my synthesis always started from power-up. On the reference emulator it showed in about 20 of 347 utterances, by up to 27 codes. With two extreme stored states in my synthesis, 15 of the 243 test inputs depended on the predecessor, by up to 80 codes. **Resolved in the text**, and in the encoder:
    - a faint opening (no voiced frame, every frame more than 36 dB below the loudest, followed by silence) is dropped, with its silence; this dropped 151 frames in 10 files, all noise blips at −40 to −46 dB before the speech;
    - any silence frame still before the first full voiced frame becomes an unvoiced repeat at energy 1, which keeps the zeroing (3 frames in 2 files; most such silences were already hiss fades);
    - a stop after unvoiced frames was checked and does not depend on the predecessor.

    Now no test input depends on its predecessor. `evaluate.py` measures this ("predecessor-dependent"), and the self-test checks the pattern and one encoded input.
